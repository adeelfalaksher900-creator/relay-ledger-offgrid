When the internet stops, volunteer work usually does not. Relay Ledger keeps a small team's tasks moving, without an account or a central server.

This is the actual app. The example is a community repair day. Tasks have an owner, a status, and notes for the next person. I can create a fresh board and add a task in a few seconds. Each change is saved on this device.

Now imagine two volunteers working separately. One marks the same task in progress. The other marks it done. A timestamp should not silently choose who is right.

Relay Ledger exchanges the full update history. In Exchange, I prepare an encrypted snapshot using a passphrase. I can save a handoff file, or show a repeating stream of QR packets. The passphrase is shared separately. It is never inside the QR code.

The other device collects the packets in any order. Duplicate frames are ignored. Missing frames stay visible, and nothing imports until the package is complete. A file is the fallback when a camera is slow or the board is large.

Here I open the incoming handoff. A wrong passphrase fails safely. With the right one, I see a preview before merging.

Both independent updates are preserved. The board asks for a decision. Choosing a status creates a new update that acknowledges both earlier versions. History keeps the context, and importing an older copy cannot erase the decision.

The app can reopen offline after its first complete installation. Local workspace data is plaintext; handoff encryption is a separate boundary. Names are labels, not verified identities. Physical camera performance still needs device testing.

Built for OFFGRID, Relay Ledger combines a causal event model, authenticated encryption, real QR decoding, and deliberate merge review. The code is open source. Our next step is testing these handoffs with small volunteer teams.

Keep the work moving. Even when the connection stops.
